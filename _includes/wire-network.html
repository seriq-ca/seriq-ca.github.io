{%- comment -%} The home title's wire network. Inline SVG rather than a stack of gradients: a graph needs branches and turns, and the two colours have to follow the theme, which a background-image cannot do. Purely decorative, so it is hidden from assistive tech. Styled in _sass/_decorations.scss. Geometry, in viewBox units, which are CSS pixels at full width. The bus runs at y=118 between two risers that turn up at x=6 and x=1018 and return inward along the top, bracketing the title between them; the network is pulled up over the title by a negative margin, so the risers run alongside it rather than under it. Under the centre of the bus hangs the hub — one oversized node, SERIQ — and everything else leaves it. A secondary bus runs out from each side of the hub at y=136; taps drop off it to y=152, and two of those turn and run again before ending, so the fan branches three deep. The levels are 16 to 18 units apart: any closer and they read as one thick band rather than as a network once the SVG is scaled down on a narrow screen. Every path is written outward from the hub — the frame in two halves meeting at the stem, every branch starting where it taps — because the draw-in dashes each one from its own start point. Reverse a d and that run grows the wrong way. Every segment is orthogonal; every node is 6 units centred on a 2-unit run, so centres land on whole units. The third level and the taps that feed it carry .wire-net__detail: below the wide breakpoint the SVG has scaled down far enough that they silt up into one smudge, so they are dropped and the frame, hub and secondary buses carry the figure alone. --i is the element's place in that outward order and --dur is how long its run takes to draw; the delays come off them in _decorations.scss. pathLength="1" normalises every run to a length of 1, so the animation can dash them without measuring a path. {%- endcomment -%}
